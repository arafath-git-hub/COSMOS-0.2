Inside the Planet

Understanding Mercury

Despite its small size, Mercury preserves important clues about the formation and evolution of rocky planets.

A Small World with a Large Iron Core

Mercury is a terrestrial planet made of rock and metal. A large metallic core occupies much of its interior, making Mercury unusually dense for its size.

Its ancient surface is covered in impact craters, broad plains and long cliffs. These features record billions of years of collisions and gradual changes to the planet's crust.

Mercury also has a global magnetic field, although it is much weaker than Earth's. Scientists use this field to investigate the planet's interior and its interaction with the solar wind.

World of Extremes

What Makes Mercury Unique?

Mercury's unusual orbit, ancient terrain and extreme environment make it one of the most fascinating rocky planets.

01 / ORBIT

The Fastest Orbit

Mercury travels around the Sun in only 88 Earth days. Its average orbital speed is about 47 kilometres per second, faster than that of any other planet.

02 / TEMPERATURE

Extreme Temperature Swings

Daytime temperatures can reach about 430°C, while nighttime temperatures may fall to around −180°C because the planet has almost no atmosphere to retain heat.

03 / GEOLOGY

An Ancient Cratered Surface

Countless impact craters cover Mercury. The Caloris Basin is one of its largest known impact structures, created by an enormous collision early in the planet's history.

04 / INTERIOR

A Massive Metallic Core

Mercury has a proportionally large iron-rich core. Its structure provides clues about how terrestrial planets formed and evolved.

05 / MAGNETISM

A Global Magnetic Field

Mercury has a weak intrinsic magnetic field. Studying its magnetosphere helps scientists understand the planet's interior and the effects of the solar wind.

06 / ICE

Water Ice Near the Sun

Permanently shadowed craters near Mercury's poles contain evidence of water ice. Some crater floors remain cold despite the planet's proximity to the Sun.

Geological Landscape

A Surface Shaped by Time

Mercury's landscape preserves a record of ancient impacts, volcanic activity and the slow contraction of its interior.

A World of Craters and Cliffs

Mercury's surface resembles Earth's Moon in many ways. It is heavily cratered, with older regions preserving evidence of collisions from the early Solar System.

Large impact basins, smooth volcanic plains and long lobate scarps reveal a complicated geological past. The scarps formed as Mercury's interior cooled and the planet contracted.

At the poles, deep craters can remain permanently shaded. Spacecraft observations indicate that water ice is preserved in some of these extremely cold regions.

The Space Environment

Mercury's Thin Exosphere

Mercury does not have a substantial atmosphere like Earth. Instead, it is surrounded by an extremely thin exosphere.

Almost a Vacuum

Atoms near Mercury can be released from the surface by sunlight, energetic particles and impacts from tiny meteoroids. These atoms form a very thin exosphere rather than a dense atmosphere.

Because the exosphere is so thin, it cannot efficiently transport heat from the sunlit side to the dark side. This contributes to Mercury's extreme temperature differences.

Robotic Exploration

Humanity's Journey to Mercury

Reaching Mercury is difficult because a spacecraft must manage the Sun's strong gravity and intense radiation environment.

Major Exploration Mission

MESSENGER

NASA's MESSENGER spacecraft became the first spacecraft to orbit Mercury in 2011. It mapped the planet, studied its magnetic field and exosphere, and gathered evidence about polar water ice. The mission concluded in 2015 when the spacecraft impacted Mercury.

Explore Missions ↗

Mariner 10

NASA's Mariner 10 spacecraft flew past Mercury three times in 1974–1975. It returned the first close-up images of the planet and discovered its weak magnetic field.

BepiColombo

The European Space Agency and Japan Aerospace Exploration Agency developed BepiColombo to investigate Mercury's surface, interior, magnetic field and interaction with the solar wind. Its mission will expand our understanding of this difficult-to-reach world.
