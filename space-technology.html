Research Field 09

Space Technology

Space technology makes it possible to explore distant worlds, observe the universe, communicate across enormous distances, and study Earth from orbit. Discover the machines and engineering that turn scientific questions into real space missions.

What Is Space Technology?

Space technology includes the spacecraft, instruments, computers, communication systems, launch vehicles, and robotic systems designed to operate in space. These systems must work in demanding conditions, including extreme temperatures, radiation, and long periods without direct human assistance.

Engineers and scientists work together to design reliable equipment, test it on Earth, launch it safely, and receive useful data from missions millions or even billions of kilometres away.

Major Areas of Space Technology

🚀 Launch Vehicles

Rockets carry satellites, scientific instruments, crewed spacecraft, and robotic explorers beyond Earth's atmosphere.

🛰️ Satellites

Satellites support weather forecasting, navigation, telecommunications, Earth observation, and scientific research.

🔭 Space Telescopes

Telescopes in space observe wavelengths that Earth's atmosphere can block or distort, helping us study stars, galaxies, and planets.

🤖 Robotic Explorers

Rovers, landers, orbiters, and robotic arms explore places that are difficult or dangerous for humans to reach.

📡 Communication Systems

Antennas and radio systems transmit commands to spacecraft and return images, measurements, and other scientific data to Earth.

🧭 Navigation and Guidance

Sensors, onboard computers, and control systems help spacecraft maintain their orientation and follow planned trajectories.

How Does a Spacecraft Work?

  • Power: Solar panels or other power systems provide electricity for onboard equipment.
  • Instruments: Cameras, spectrometers, and sensors collect scientific observations.
  • Communication: Antennas send information to Earth and receive commands from mission controllers.
  • Thermal control: Insulation, radiators, and heaters help keep sensitive equipment within safe operating temperatures.
  • Flight control: Thrusters, reaction wheels, and navigation systems help control movement and orientation.
  • Onboard computing: Computers manage spacecraft operations and can respond to certain problems automatically.

Technologies That Changed Space Exploration

Hubble Space Telescope

Launched in 1990, Hubble has produced detailed observations of planets, nebulae, stars, and galaxies.

James Webb Space Telescope

Webb observes mainly infrared light, helping scientists investigate early galaxies, star formation, and planetary atmospheres.

Mars Rovers

Robotic vehicles such as Curiosity and Perseverance examine Martian rocks, soil, climate, and evidence of ancient environments.

Voyager Spacecraft

Voyager 1 and Voyager 2 explored the outer planets and continue transmitting limited scientific information from interstellar space.

Reusable Rockets

Reusing certain rocket components can reduce the need to build entirely new hardware for every launch.

Autonomous Systems

Spacecraft use onboard software and sensors to perform tasks when communication delays make constant control from Earth impractical.

Why Space Technology Matters

Space technology expands scientific knowledge and also provides practical benefits on Earth. Satellite systems support disaster monitoring, weather observations, environmental research, and global communication. Technologies developed for space can also inspire advances in materials, sensors, robotics, and computing.
