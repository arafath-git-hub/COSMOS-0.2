01 / Cosmic Foundations

Ancient pieces of a young Solar System.

Asteroids and comets preserve clues about the material from which planets formed. Their different compositions and orbits help reveal the Solar System's history.

01 — ASTEROIDS

Rocky and metallic bodies

Asteroids are small Solar System bodies that are commonly rocky or metallic. Many orbit the Sun in the main asteroid belt between Mars and Jupiter.

02 — COMETS

Icy travellers

Comets contain ice, dust and rocky material. Near the Sun, heating can release gas and dust to form a coma and sometimes spectacular tails.

03 — METEOROIDS

Small fragments

Small pieces of rock or metal moving through space are called meteoroids. When one produces a bright streak in an atmosphere, the visible event is a meteor.

03 / Compare the Objects

Asteroids vs. comets.

The distinction is useful, but real small Solar System bodies can be diverse and do not always fit a simple category.

ROCKY OR METALLIC

Asteroids

Composition

Often rock, metal or carbon-rich material; compositions vary widely.

Where found

Many occupy the main asteroid belt, while others have near-Earth or other orbital paths.

Activity

Most do not form large comas and tails, although some active asteroids release dust.

ICE, DUST AND ROCK

Comets

Composition

Mixtures of ice, dust and rocky material, with properties that vary from comet to comet.

Where found

Many originate in distant reservoirs such as the Kuiper Belt or Oort Cloud.

Activity

Solar heating can release gas and dust, producing a coma and one or more tails.

These are broad patterns, not absolute rules. Some asteroids show comet-like activity, and some objects have properties that blur the traditional distinction.
04 / Planetary Defense

Finding and tracking near-Earth objects.

Planetary defense combines observation, orbital calculations and international scientific coordination.

STEP 01

Detect

Telescopes repeatedly image the sky to identify moving objects against the background of distant stars.

STEP 02

Calculate

Scientists use observations to estimate an object's orbit, size and possible future approaches. Additional observations improve uncertain measurements.

STEP 03

Assess and Prepare

Researchers evaluate potential impact scenarios. Missions such as DART have tested one possible technique for changing an object's orbit.

A close approach does not automatically mean an impact threat. Risk assessment depends on the object's measured orbit, uncertainty and future trajectory. Consult current official monitoring data for live assessments.
05 / Exploration Milestones

Spacecraft meet small worlds.

Robotic missions have transformed asteroids and comets from distant points of light into worlds that can be mapped and studied directly.

1986

Halley's Comet Encounters

Several spacecraft from different space agencies studied Halley's Comet during its return to the inner Solar System.

2001

NEAR Shoemaker at Eros

NASA's NEAR Shoemaker spacecraft completed a historic landing on asteroid Eros after orbiting and studying it.

2014

Rosetta at Comet 67P

ESA's Rosetta reached comet 67P and studied its nucleus and activity during its journey around the Sun.

2018–2020

Hayabusa2 and Ryugu

JAXA's Hayabusa2 explored asteroid Ryugu and returned samples to Earth.

2021–2023

DART and OSIRIS-REx

DART tested asteroid deflection in 2022. OSIRIS-REx returned its Bennu sample capsule to Earth in 2023.

Ongoing

Monitoring and Future Exploration

Ground-based surveys and spacecraft continue to study small bodies, improve orbit predictions and investigate the origins of the Solar System.

06 / Evidence Check

Facts worth knowing.

Small bodies help researchers understand planetary formation and assess natural impact hazards.

Comet tails do not simply trail behind the comet.

Dust and ion tails are influenced by sunlight and the solar wind, so they generally point away from the Sun.

The asteroid belt is mostly empty space.

Although it contains many objects, the distances between individual asteroids are generally enormous.

Meteors and meteorites are different.

A meteor is the visible atmospheric event. A meteorite is material that survives and reaches the ground.

Not every near-Earth asteroid is hazardous.

Near-Earth describes an orbital relationship; a potential impact requires a separate assessment of the object's trajectory.

Asteroids preserve ancient material.

Some asteroid samples contain primitive material that helps scientists investigate the early Solar System.

Spacecraft can change an asteroid's motion.

NASA's DART mission demonstrated a measurable orbital change after a spacecraft deliberately impacted Dimorphos.

07 / Knowledge Check

Questions about asteroids and comets.

Understand the difference between small bodies, atmospheric phenomena and impact risks.

An asteroid is a small Solar System body that orbits the Sun. Many are rocky or metallic, and many occupy the main asteroid belt between Mars and Jupiter.
Comets contain mixtures of ice, dust and rocky material. When a comet approaches the Sun, released gas and dust can form a coma and tails.
Solar heating releases gas and dust from a comet's surface. Sunlight pressure and the solar wind help shape the tails, which generally point away from the Sun.
They are different categories of small Solar System bodies, often distinguished by composition and activity. However, some objects display properties that blur the distinction.
A near-Earth object, or NEO, is an asteroid or comet whose orbit brings it into Earth's orbital neighbourhood. NEO status alone does not mean that the object will hit Earth.
A meteor is the light phenomenon produced when a meteoroid enters an atmosphere. A meteorite is a surviving fragment that reaches the ground.
DART demonstrated that a kinetic impactor can change a small body's orbit. Any real response would depend on advance warning, object characteristics, orbit uncertainty and international planning.
OSIRIS-REx returned samples from asteroid Bennu in 2023. Laboratory analysis of those samples allows researchers to investigate the asteroid's composition and early Solar System history.
Many long-period comets are thought to originate in the distant Oort Cloud, a vast hypothesised reservoir of icy bodies surrounding the Solar System.
Surveys take repeated images of the sky and search for moving points of light. Follow-up observations help determine an object's orbit and physical properties.
Asteroids preserve clues about planetary formation and early Solar System chemistry. Studying their orbits and composition also helps scientists assess impact hazards.
NASA's Center for Near-Earth Object Studies provides orbit and close approach information. Use official scientific monitoring sources for current data rather than relying on unsourced social media claims.
