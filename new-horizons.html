01 / Mission Overview

A Journey to the Solar System's Frontier

New Horizons was designed to investigate Pluto and its moons, then continue into the Kuiper Belt, a distant region containing primitive icy bodies left over from the formation of the Solar System.

Why This Mission Matters

Before New Horizons arrived, Pluto had only been observed from great distances. The spacecraft's 2015 flyby provided the first close-up observations of its surface, atmosphere and surrounding system.

These observations revealed a world with mountains of water ice, broad plains of nitrogen ice, complex geological features and a surprisingly varied landscape.

The mission later visited Arrokoth, a small Kuiper Belt object, offering a closer look at a primitive body from the early Solar System.

02 / Mission Timeline

Milestones Across Deep Space

From launch to the outer Solar System, each milestone expanded humanity's view of distant worlds.

2006

Launch from Earth

New Horizons launched on 19 January 2006, beginning its journey toward Pluto and the Kuiper Belt.

2007

Jupiter Flyby

The spacecraft passed Jupiter and used its gravity to gain speed while making scientific observations of the giant planet.

2015

Historic Pluto Encounter

On 14 July 2015, New Horizons made its closest approach to Pluto, returning detailed images and measurements of Pluto and its moons.

2019

Arrokoth Flyby

The spacecraft flew past Arrokoth on 1 January 2019. The encounter provided close-up information about a small, primitive object in the Kuiper Belt.

Beyond 2019

Continuing Through the Kuiper Belt

After the Arrokoth encounter, New Horizons continued studying the distant environment and other objects observable from its trajectory, subject to mission resources and operational plans.

03 / Scientific Discoveries

What New Horizons Revealed

Its observations showed that the outer Solar System is more diverse and geologically interesting than scientists once expected.

DISCOVERY 01

Pluto's Complex Surface

Pluto contains mountains, craters, icy plains and varied surface regions, demonstrating a complex geological history.

DISCOVERY 02

The Heart-Shaped Region

The bright region informally known as Tombaugh Regio includes Sputnik Planitia, a large plain dominated by nitrogen ice.

DISCOVERY 03

Pluto's Thin Atmosphere

Observations revealed atmospheric hazes and helped researchers investigate the structure and behavior of Pluto's atmosphere.

DISCOVERY 04

Charon and Pluto's Moons

Images and measurements provided new views of Charon, Pluto's largest moon, along with its smaller moons.

DISCOVERY 05

Arrokoth's Primitive Structure

Arrokoth's two connected lobes provided evidence about how some small bodies may have gently assembled in the early Solar System.

DISCOVERY 06

The Distant Solar System

The mission expanded knowledge of icy bodies beyond Neptune and offered a rare close-up study of the Kuiper Belt.

Why the Kuiper Belt Is Important

The Kuiper Belt contains many small icy bodies beyond Neptune. Because some of these objects preserve material from the Solar System's early history, studying them helps scientists investigate how planets and smaller bodies formed and evolved.

04 / Spacecraft Technology

A Robotic Explorer Built for the Distance

New Horizons carries scientific instruments designed to collect images, analyze particles and measure the environment far from the Sun.

Scientific Instruments

The spacecraft's instrument suite includes imaging systems, spectrometers and particle and dust detectors. Together, these instruments help scientists study surface features, atmospheric properties, energetic particles and the distant space environment.

Its long-distance operations depend on careful spacecraft management and communication with Earth.

How It Communicates

New Horizons uses a high-gain antenna to communicate with Earth across enormous distances. Signals take hours to travel one way between the spacecraft and Earth, depending on their separation.

Because of this delay, mission operations cannot rely on instantaneous control. The spacecraft must execute carefully planned commands and onboard procedures.

05 / Mission Legacy

Changing Our View of Distant Worlds

New Horizons showed that Pluto is a scientifically rich world rather than simply a distant dot in a telescope. Its exploration of Arrokoth also provided a rare look at a small object that has remained in the cold outer Solar System for billions of years.

These discoveries continue to help researchers understand planetary geology, the evolution of atmospheres and the processes that shaped the early Solar System.

The mission represents an important step in humanity's continuing exploration of the distant planetary frontier.
