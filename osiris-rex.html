Asteroid Sample Return

OSIRIS-REx

NASA's OSIRIS-REx mission traveled to asteroid Bennu, collected a sample of its surface material, and delivered that sample to Earth. By studying the material in laboratories, scientists can investigate the early Solar System and the ingredients that helped form planets.

Space Agency NASA
Launch Date September 8, 2016
Target Asteroid 101955 Bennu
Sample Returned September 24, 2023

What Was OSIRIS-REx?

OSIRIS-REx stands for Origins, Spectral Interpretation, Resource Identification, and Security–Regolith Explorer. It was a robotic spacecraft developed to map asteroid Bennu, investigate its properties, collect surface material, and return the sample to Earth.

Bennu is a small, carbon-rich near-Earth asteroid. Its material preserves clues about the substances present when the Solar System formed billions of years ago.

Mission Timeline

  • 2016: OSIRIS-REx launched from Earth.
  • 2018: The spacecraft arrived at Bennu and began detailed mapping.
  • 2020: It touched the asteroid's surface with its sampling mechanism and collected material.
  • 2021: The spacecraft departed Bennu and began its return journey.
  • 2023: The sample capsule landed in Utah, USA, delivering asteroid material to Earth.
  • After sample delivery: The spacecraft continued into an extended mission called OSIRIS-APEX to study asteroid Apophis.

How Did It Collect the Sample?

OSIRIS-REx used a system called Touch-And-Go Sample Acquisition Mechanism, or TAGSAM. The spacecraft briefly contacted Bennu's surface and released a burst of nitrogen gas to stir up surface material. The collection head captured some of this material.

The sample was stored in a return capsule, which separated from the spacecraft and descended to Earth by parachute in September 2023.

Unlike missions that only send images and measurements, OSIRIS-REx returned physical asteroid material for scientists to examine directly in laboratories.

What Can Scientists Learn?

  • Which minerals and chemical compounds occur in Bennu's material.
  • How carbon-rich asteroids formed and changed over time.
  • Whether the sample contains organic molecules and water-related minerals.
  • What small bodies may have delivered to the early Earth.
  • How scientists can better characterize near-Earth asteroids.

Finding organic molecules does not by itself prove that life existed on Bennu. These substances can also form through non-biological chemical processes.

Why Is Bennu Important?

Bennu's orbit brings it relatively close to Earth, making it important for asteroid research. Studying its physical structure and composition helps scientists improve their understanding of asteroid evolution and assess the properties of potentially hazardous near-Earth objects.

Mission Status

The original OSIRIS-REx sample-return mission has been completed. Its spacecraft continues as OSIRIS-APEX, a separate extended mission that is planned to investigate asteroid Apophis after its close approach to Earth in 2029. Check NASA's official page for current mission updates.
