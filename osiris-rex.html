OSIRIS-REx Return From Bennu

A landmark NASA mission that travelled to a near-Earth asteroid, collected a sample from its surface, and delivered ancient material to Earth for scientists to study.

Space Agency NASA

01 / Mission Overview

A Journey Into the Early Solar System

OSIRIS-REx stands for Origins, Spectral Interpretation, Resource Identification, and Security–Regolith Explorer. Its main objective was to study asteroid Bennu and bring a sample back to Earth.

Why Bennu?

Bennu is a carbon-rich asteroid that preserves material from the Solar System's early history. Studying it helps scientists investigate the ingredients that existed when planets formed.

A Historic Achievement

The mission collected material from Bennu and delivered it to Earth in 2023. It was NASA's first mission to return a sample from an asteroid.

02 / Mission Timeline

From Launch to Sample Return

Years of navigation, asteroid mapping, surface operations and sample analysis made this mission possible.

2016

Launch

OSIRIS-REx launched on September 8, beginning its journey toward asteroid Bennu.

2018

Arrival at Bennu

The spacecraft arrived in December and began detailed observations and mapping of the asteroid.

2020

Sample Collection

On October 20, the spacecraft used its TAGSAM mechanism to collect surface material from Bennu.

2021

Journey Back Begins

The spacecraft departed Bennu in May, carrying the sealed sample capsule toward Earth.

2023

Sample Delivered

The sample capsule landed in Utah on September 24. The main spacecraft continued its journey under the OSIRIS-APEX mission name.

2029

Planned Apophis Encounter

OSIRIS-APEX is scheduled to reach asteroid Apophis in 2029 and study how its close approach to Earth affects the asteroid's surface.

03 / Sample Technology

How Did It Collect the Sample?

Rather than landing like a conventional spacecraft, OSIRIS-REx briefly touched Bennu's surface and collected loose material.

Touch-and-Go System

The spacecraft used a robotic sampling arm called TAGSAM. It contacted the surface for a short period and released a burst of nitrogen gas to stir up and capture regolith.

Sealed for the Journey

The collected material was secured inside a sample return capsule. That capsule separated from the spacecraft and returned to Earth in September 2023.

The result

NASA reported approximately 121.6 grams of Bennu material recovered from the sample return capsule—more than the mission's 60-gram minimum requirement.

04 / Scientific Value

What Can Scientists Learn?

Samples can be examined with laboratory instruments far more powerful than those that can be carried aboard a spacecraft.

Origins of the Solar System

Minerals and chemical compounds in Bennu can reveal conditions that existed billions of years ago, before the planets reached their present form.

Water and Organic Chemistry

Bennu samples contain water-bearing minerals and carbon-rich material. Their analysis helps researchers investigate chemical processes that may have contributed to the ingredients for life.

Asteroid Structure

Combining the sample with spacecraft observations helps researchers understand how Bennu formed and how its surface material is distributed.

Planetary Defense

Studying Bennu's composition, orbit and physical properties improves our understanding of near-Earth asteroids and the challenges involved in assessing their future trajectories.

05 / The Target

Why Is Bennu Important?

Bennu is a small near-Earth asteroid whose orbit brings it relatively close to our planet. Scientists study its trajectory to improve long-term risk assessments, while its ancient carbon-rich material offers a window into the chemistry of the early Solar System.

Bennu is not predicted to collide with Earth during the mission's operational period. Its long-term orbital evolution is monitored because small changes in an asteroid's motion can matter over extended timescales.

06 / Current Mission

OSIRIS-REx Has Evolved Into OSIRIS-APEX

Sample Returned; Extended Mission Underway

The original OSIRIS-REx sample-return objective is complete. The spacecraft was redirected to study asteroid Apophis and continues as OSIRIS-APEX. Its planned arrival at Apophis is in 2029.
