02 / World profile

A remarkably connected planet

Earth's atmosphere, oceans, interior and living systems interact to shape a constantly changing world.

PROFILE / A

The water world

Roughly 71% of Earth's surface is covered by water. Oceans store heat, influence weather and climate, and support enormous ecosystems. Water also moves continuously through the atmosphere, land and sea in the global water cycle.

PROFILE / B

A protective atmosphere

Earth's atmosphere is composed mainly of nitrogen and oxygen. It helps regulate surface temperatures, supplies gases essential to life and reduces exposure to some harmful solar radiation.

03 / Planetary systems

What makes Earth special?

SYSTEM 001

Liquid water

Stable liquid water at the surface is central to Earth's ecosystems and is one reason the planet is so important in the search for life beyond Earth.

SYSTEM 002

Magnetic field

Motion in Earth's liquid outer core generates a magnetic field that helps deflect many charged particles arriving from the Sun.

SYSTEM 003

Active geology

Moving tectonic plates reshape continents and ocean floors, build mountains, generate earthquakes and help recycle material through Earth's interior.

Surface environment

71%

Approximately the fraction of Earth's surface covered by water

One planet, many environments

From deep ocean trenches to high mountain ranges, Earth contains an extraordinary range of environments. Sunlight, atmospheric circulation, ocean currents and geology interact to influence regional climates.

Studying these connected systems helps researchers understand natural hazards, monitor environmental change and improve predictions of future conditions.

04 / Life and ecosystems

A living world

Life influences Earth's environment, while the planet's conditions make an enormous diversity of life possible.

BIOSPHERE / 01

Life in many forms

Organisms live in oceans, forests, deserts, polar regions and even extreme environments. Scientists study this biodiversity to understand how life evolves and how ecosystems respond to environmental pressures.

BIOSPHERE / 02

A changing climate

Earth's climate varies naturally, but human activities, especially greenhouse-gas emissions, are driving modern global warming. Satellite observations help monitor temperatures, ice, oceans, vegetation and atmospheric conditions.

06 / Research frontiers

Questions scientists are investigating

How will Earth's climate change?

Observations and climate models help researchers estimate future changes in temperature, rainfall, sea level and extreme weather.

How do ecosystems respond?

Scientists investigate how species, habitats and food webs respond to warming, pollution, land-use changes and other environmental pressures.

What lies beneath Earth's surface?

Seismic waves, magnetic measurements and geological studies reveal clues about Earth's deep interior and its ongoing evolution.

How can satellites protect communities?

Satellite data helps monitor storms, floods, wildfires, droughts and other hazards, supporting research and disaster preparedness.
