NEPTUNE

The Blue Frontier

Far beyond the orbit of Uranus lies a world of deep blue clouds, supersonic winds and mysterious storms. Neptune is a distant ice giant that continues to challenge our understanding of planetary atmospheres and the outer Solar System.

A world of extremes

Inside the blue giant

Neptune may look calm from a distance, but its atmosphere and interior contain some of the most extreme conditions among the planets.

01 / ATMOSPHERE

A deep blue atmosphere

Hydrogen and helium dominate Neptune's atmosphere, with methane also present. Methane absorbs red light, contributing to the planet's blue appearance. Other atmospheric factors also influence its exact color.

02 / WEATHER

Violent planetary winds

Neptune's atmosphere hosts extremely fast winds and changing cloud patterns. Despite receiving little sunlight, it remains a dynamic world with powerful weather systems.

03 / INTERIOR

An ice giant's interior

Beneath its atmosphere, Neptune is thought to contain a hot, dense interior rich in water, ammonia and methane under extreme pressure. These materials behave very differently from ordinary ice.

04 / ORBIT

A distant solar orbit

Neptune takes roughly 165 Earth years to complete one trip around the Sun. Its great distance makes it a key world for understanding the farthest reaches of our planetary system.

05 / RINGS

Dark, faint rings

Neptune has a faint ring system made of dusty material. Some of its rings contain brighter clumps known as ring arcs, which make this distant system especially intriguing.

06 / MAGNETISM

A tilted magnetic field

Neptune's magnetic field is strongly tilted relative to its rotation axis and offset from the planet's center. Its unusual geometry offers clues about the planet's deep interior.

Atmospheric phenomenon

Dark storms in a blue world

Neptune can develop enormous dark vortices in its atmosphere. Voyager 2 observed the Great Dark Spot during its 1989 flyby. Unlike Jupiter's long-lived Great Red Spot, Neptune's dark spots can appear and disappear over time.

Studying these storms helps scientists understand how heat, atmospheric circulation and chemistry shape ice giant planets.

ATMOSPHERIC SYSTEM / NEPTUNE
Natural satellites

Meet Neptune's moons

Neptune's moons have varied histories, from a large captured satellite with a retrograde orbit to smaller bodies traveling closer to the planet.

Triton

Neptune's largest moon follows a retrograde orbit, suggesting it was captured from the outer Solar System. Voyager 2 observed nitrogen geysers on its surface.

Nereid

Nereid follows an unusually elongated orbit. Its distant path makes it one of Neptune's most distinctive known satellites.

Proteus

Proteus is one of Neptune's largest known moons. Its irregular shape and heavily cratered surface provide clues to its history.

Hippocamp

A small inner moon discovered in images from the Hubble Space Telescope. It may be connected to material ejected from a larger moon.

Humanity's exploration

Missions to Neptune

Neptune has been explored at close range by only one spacecraft. Telescopes continue to reveal changes in its atmosphere from Earth and from space.

1989

Voyager 2

The first and only spacecraft to fly past Neptune. It studied the planet's atmosphere, rings, magnetic field and moons, including Triton.

Frontiers of science

What scientists still want to know

Neptune remains one of the least explored planets. Future dedicated missions could answer questions that remote observations cannot fully resolve.

01

What drives its extreme winds?

Researchers are investigating how internal heat and atmospheric circulation maintain Neptune's powerful weather.

02

How do dark spots form?

Tracking atmospheric vortices can reveal how storms develop, change shape and eventually disappear.

03

What lies beneath the clouds?

Models of Neptune's interior aim to explain its composition, heat flow, magnetic field and unusual physical conditions.

04

Why is Triton so unusual?

Studying Triton's geology and atmosphere could reveal how it formed and became part of Neptune's system.
