MARSThe Red Planet

A cold desert world with giant volcanoes, ancient river valleys and a thin atmosphere. Mars preserves clues that liquid water once flowed across its surface — and it remains a major target in the search for evidence of ancient life beyond Earth.

A small planet with a huge story

Mars is the fourth planet from the Sun. Its red appearance comes largely from iron minerals in its dust and rocks that have oxidized, producing rust-like compounds.

02 / Planet profile

Inside the Martian world

Mars has a rocky surface, a thin carbon-dioxide atmosphere, polar ice and a landscape shaped by impacts, wind and ancient water-related processes.

Atmosphere

A thin veil of gas

Mars's atmosphere is much thinner than Earth's and is composed mostly of carbon dioxide. Its low pressure means liquid water cannot normally remain stable for long on the open surface under present-day conditions.

  • Main gasCarbon dioxide (CO₂)
  • Surface pressureLess than 1% of Earth's, typically
  • WeatherWinds, dust devils and dust storms
  • CloudsWater-ice and carbon-dioxide clouds
Surface & geology

A landscape of extremes

Mars features ancient impact craters, enormous volcanoes, deep canyons, dunes and dried-up river channels. The planet's surface records a long history of geological change, although the timing and causes of many events remain under study.

  • Olympus MonsLargest known volcano in the Solar System
  • Valles MarinerisVast canyon system
  • Surface colourIron-rich oxidized dust
  • Geological activityPast activity is well documented
Water & ice

Clues from a wetter past

Ancient river channels, lake deposits and minerals that form in the presence of water show that liquid water once played an important role on Mars. Today, water is found mainly as ice, including at the polar caps and beneath parts of the surface.

  • Polar regionsWater ice and seasonal CO₂ ice
  • Ancient evidenceChannels, deltas and hydrated minerals
  • Modern surface waterNot stable for long under typical conditions
  • Subsurface iceDetected in multiple regions
Interior & magnetic field

What lies beneath the surface?

Mars has a crust, mantle and metallic core. Unlike Earth, it does not have a strong global magnetic field today, though parts of its crust preserve magnetism from an earlier era. NASA's InSight lander used seismic measurements to investigate the planet's interior.

  • Interior layersCrust, mantle and core
  • Global magnetic fieldNo strong global field today
  • Ancient crustContains magnetized regions
  • Interior investigationInSight seismic science
Did Mars once have rivers and lakes? Strong geological evidence shows that water flowed and collected in some ancient Martian environments. That does not prove life existed there, but it helps scientists identify places where ancient microbial life might have been possible.
03 / Martian landmarks

Three extraordinary places

Mars is home to some of the most dramatic landscapes in the Solar System.

The giant volcano

Olympus Mons

Olympus Mons is a vast shield volcano built by repeated lava flows. It rises roughly 22 kilometres above the surrounding plains when measured from its base, making it the tallest known volcano in the Solar System by that measure.

The giant canyon

Valles Marineris

This immense canyon system stretches thousands of kilometres across Mars. It is far longer and much wider than the Grand Canyon on Earth, and its formation likely involved multiple geological processes.

The ancient lake region

Jezero Crater

Jezero Crater contains evidence of an ancient lake and river delta. NASA's Perseverance rover explores its rocks and collects samples to help scientists investigate ancient environments and possible signs of past microbial life.

The polar regions

Ice caps

Mars has polar caps that change with the seasons. Water ice remains in the polar regions, while carbon dioxide can freeze out of the atmosphere during the coldest periods.

04 / The search for life

Could Mars ever have supported life?

Scientists have not confirmed life on Mars. The strongest approach is to investigate ancient environments and search for evidence that can be tested carefully.

Habitability

Ancient lake beds, water-altered minerals and sedimentary rocks help scientists reconstruct whether Mars once had environments that could support microbial life.

Biosignatures

Researchers look for patterns in minerals, organic molecules and rock textures that might preserve evidence of life. No single suggestive feature is enough to confirm it.

Sample return

Returning carefully selected Martian samples to Earth would allow laboratories to perform detailed analyses that are difficult or impossible for a rover to carry out on Mars. The future of sample-return plans depends on mission decisions and funding.

05 / Robotic explorers

The machines exploring Mars

Orbiters map the planet from above, landers study particular locations, and rovers travel across the surface to examine rocks and soil.

1997

Sojourner

NASA's small rover became the first successful wheeled rover to operate on Mars, demonstrating a new way to investigate the planet's surface.

First Mars rover
2004

Spirit & Opportunity

These twin rovers investigated rocks and soils and found geological evidence that liquid water had affected parts of Mars in the past.

Past water evidence
2012

Curiosity

Curiosity explores Gale Crater, studying rocks, minerals and environmental chemistry to understand whether ancient Mars could have supported microbial life.

Habitability research
2018

InSight

NASA's lander studied Mars's interior using seismic measurements and heat-flow investigations. Its science mission ended in 2022 after dust accumulation prevented sufficient power generation.

Interior science
2021

Perseverance

Perseverance explores Jezero Crater, studies ancient rocks and collects samples for possible future analysis on Earth. It also carried the Ingenuity helicopter technology demonstration.

Rocks and samples
2021

Ingenuity

Ingenuity demonstrated powered, controlled flight on another planet. After completing 72 flights, it sustained rotor damage during its final flight in January 2024 and ended operations.

First powered flight on Mars
06 / Future exploration

What comes next for Mars science?

Future missions may improve our understanding of the planet's climate history, interior, resources and potential habitability. Plans and launch dates can change.

More sample science

Samples collected by Perseverance could provide valuable evidence about ancient Martian environments if a feasible return mission is approved and carried out.

Human exploration

Research into radiation, dust, habitats, life-support systems and local resources will be important for any future human mission to Mars.

Atmospheric evolution

Studying how Mars lost much of its atmosphere can help explain how its climate changed and why the planet became so cold and dry.
