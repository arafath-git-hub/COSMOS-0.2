MARS

The Red Planet

A cold desert world marked by ancient river valleys, enormous volcanoes and a thin atmosphere. Mars preserves clues to a wetter past and remains one of the most important destinations in the search for ancient life beyond Earth.

02 / World profile

A desert with a deep history

Mars is cold and dry today, but its surface contains evidence that liquid water flowed there in the distant past.

PROFILE / A

Evidence of ancient water

Orbital observations and rover investigations have identified dried river channels, ancient lake environments and minerals that formed in the presence of water. These discoveries help scientists reconstruct Mars's earlier climate.

PROFILE / B

A thin atmosphere

Mars has a very thin atmosphere made mostly of carbon dioxide. Its low atmospheric pressure makes stable liquid water difficult to maintain on the surface under present-day conditions.

03 / Planetary systems

What makes Mars extraordinary?

FEATURE 001

Olympus Mons

Mars hosts Olympus Mons, a giant shield volcano that rises roughly 22 kilometres above the surrounding terrain and is among the largest volcanoes known in the Solar System.

FEATURE 002

Valles Marineris

This immense canyon system stretches thousands of kilometres across the planet, making it one of the most striking geological features in the Solar System.

FEATURE 003

Dust storms

Fine surface dust can be lifted into the atmosphere. Regional storms sometimes grow into planet-encircling events that alter the amount of sunlight reaching the ground.

The search for ancient life

Ancient Water

A major clue in the investigation of Mars's past habitability

Could Mars once have supported microbial life?

Ancient river channels, lake deposits and water-altered minerals show that some Martian environments were once much wetter. On Earth, similar environments can preserve evidence of microbial life.

Scientists are studying Martian rocks and sediments for signs of past habitability. Evidence that life actually existed on Mars has not yet been confirmed.

04 / Surface exploration

A planet explored by robots

Orbiters, landers and rovers provide different perspectives on Mars, from global mapping to close-up rock analysis.

EXPLORATION / 01

Rovers on the surface

Robotic rovers examine rocks, analyse soil and investigate ancient environments. Their observations help scientists identify locations that may preserve evidence of Mars's past.

EXPLORATION / 02

Orbiters above the planet

Spacecraft in orbit map the surface, study atmospheric changes and relay communications for surface missions. Together, these missions build a broader picture of the planet.

06 / Research frontiers

Questions scientists are investigating

Did life ever exist on Mars?

Researchers search for preserved biosignatures while carefully distinguishing biological evidence from non-biological chemistry.

Where did the water go?

Scientists investigate how water escaped to space, became trapped in minerals or remained in possible underground reservoirs.

What lies beneath the surface?

Radar, seismic observations and geological studies help reveal information about the crust, interior and possible subsurface ice.

How could humans explore Mars?

Future exploration research includes radiation protection, life-support systems, landing technologies and ways to use local resources safely.
