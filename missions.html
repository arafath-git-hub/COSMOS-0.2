Machines that changed our view of space

Space exploration uses different tools for different questions: orbiters map worlds, landers study surfaces, rovers travel across terrain, and telescopes observe distant objects.

MISSION 01

Apollo

The Moon

NASA's Apollo program carried astronauts to the lunar surface. Apollo 11 landed humans on the Moon in July 1969 and returned samples that continue to support scientific research.

Explore Apollo ↗
MISSION 02

Voyager

Deep space

Launched in 1977, Voyager 1 and Voyager 2 explored the outer planets. Both spacecraft later crossed into interstellar space, carrying instruments that study the environment beyond the heliosphere.

Explore Voyager ↗
MISSION 03

Mars Rovers

Mars

Robotic rovers examine Martian rocks, landscapes, and past environments. Curiosity investigates habitability, while Perseverance studies ancient environments and collects samples for possible future return.

Explore Mars ↗
MISSION 04

Juno

Jupiter

Juno studies Jupiter's atmosphere, gravity, magnetic field, and interior. Its observations help scientists understand how giant planets form and evolve.

Explore Juno ↗
MISSION 05

James Webb

Space telescope

The James Webb Space Telescope observes infrared light to investigate early galaxies, star formation, planetary systems, and the atmospheres of exoplanets.

Explore Webb ↗
MISSION 06

Europa Clipper

Icy moon

NASA's Europa Clipper mission is travelling to Jupiter's system to investigate whether Europa has conditions that could support life. It is designed to perform repeated close flybys of the moon.

Explore Europa Clipper ↗
MISSION 07

OSIRIS-REx

Asteroid Bennu

OSIRIS-REx collected a sample from asteroid Bennu and delivered it to Earth in 2023. The sample helps researchers investigate the early Solar System and the chemistry of primitive asteroids.

MISSION 08

Hubble

Cosmos

The Hubble Space Telescope has observed planets, nebulae, galaxies, and distant cosmic events, producing discoveries and images that have transformed astronomy.

Lunar exploration

A timeline of discovery

Milestones in space exploration

Selected landmarks in humanity's effort to explore beyond Earth.

1957 — Sputnik 1

The first artificial satellite to orbit Earth marked the beginning of the Space Age.

1969 — Apollo 11

Humans walked on the Moon for the first time and returned lunar samples for scientific study.

1977 — Voyager launches

Two spacecraft began journeys that would transform knowledge of the outer planets and eventually reach interstellar space.

1990 — Hubble Space Telescope

Hubble began its observations from Earth orbit, producing major advances across astronomy.

2012 — Curiosity lands on Mars

The rover began investigating whether ancient Mars had environments suitable for microbial life.

2021 — James Webb launched

Webb began a new era of infrared astronomy after its journey to its observing location.

The next frontier

What will we discover next?

Future missions may investigate icy ocean worlds, return samples from Mars, study the Sun in greater detail, search for habitable exoplanets, and explore destinations humans have never visited. Every mission starts with a question—and a plan to gather evidence.
