01 / ORIGIN

The Big Bang

The universe evolved from an extremely hot, dense early state and has expanded and cooled over billions of years.

Strong observational support
02 / EARLY UNIVERSE

Cosmic Inflation

A proposed very brief period of extremely rapid expansion that could explain several large-scale properties of the universe.

Active research
03 / FUNDAMENTALS

Space-Time

In general relativity, space and time form a connected structure whose geometry is influenced by matter and energy.

General relativity
04 / INVISIBLE MATTER

Dark Matter

Evidence from galaxy motion, gravitational lensing, and cosmic structure points to matter that does not emit or absorb light in the usual way.

Nature still unknown
05 / COSMIC EXPANSION

Dark Energy

The name given to whatever drives the observed accelerated expansion of the universe. Its physical nature remains uncertain.

Open question
06 / EXTREME GRAVITY

Black Holes

Regions where gravity is so strong that inside the event horizon, nothing can escape to the distant universe, not even light.

Observed phenomena
07 / LARGE-SCALE STRUCTURE

The Cosmic Web

Galaxies and dark matter form a vast network of filaments, clusters, and enormous voids across the universe.

Mapped by surveys
08 / SPECULATIVE IDEAS

The Multiverse

Some theoretical frameworks suggest that our universe might be one of many. There is currently no conclusive observational evidence for other universes.

Speculative hypothesis
09 / COSMIC AFTERGLOW

Cosmic Microwave Background

This ancient radiation comes from the time when the universe became transparent, about 380,000 years after the Big Bang.

Precision cosmology
10 / MEASURING SPACE

Expansion and Redshift

Light from distant galaxies is stretched as space expands. Its redshift helps astronomers investigate cosmic history.

Observational evidence
11 / COSMIC HISTORY

The Early Universe

As the universe cooled, fundamental particles, light nuclei, atoms, and eventually the first stars and galaxies emerged.

Early cosmic epochs
12 / ELEMENTS

Primordial Nucleosynthesis

During the first few minutes, nuclear reactions produced much of the universe's hydrogen and helium, along with tiny amounts of other light nuclei.

Big Bang evidence
13 / GALAXIES

Galaxy Formation

Gravity gathered matter into structures that developed into galaxies, where gas, stars, dust, and black holes interact and evolve.

Cosmic evolution
14 / COSMIC MEASUREMENTS

The Hubble Constant

This parameter describes the present expansion rate. Different measurement methods produce values that do not fully agree, a problem known as the Hubble tension.

Active debate
15 / SPACE-TIME SIGNALS

Gravitational Waves

Ripples in space-time produced by accelerating massive objects, detected from events such as merging black holes and neutron stars.

New observational window
16 / THEORETICAL FRAMEWORK

The Standard Cosmological Model

The Lambda-CDM model combines general relativity with an expanding universe containing ordinary matter, cold dark matter, and a cosmological constant.

Best current framework
17 / THE DISTANT FUTURE

The Fate of the Universe

If accelerated expansion continues, the universe may grow increasingly cold and dilute. Its ultimate fate depends on the properties of dark energy.

Model-dependent predictions

Scientists are investigating dark matter, dark energy, the matter-antimatter imbalance, quantum gravity, and the earliest moments of cosmic history.

The scientific method

How do we study something we cannot visit?

Cosmologists use light, gravity, mathematical models, and observations collected across different distances and epochs. The strongest explanations are those that make testable predictions and agree with independent measurements.

Light and redshift Measure how spectra shift to estimate cosmic expansion and study distant objects.
Cosmic microwave background Study tiny temperature and polarization variations in the universe's ancient radiation.
Supernovae and standard candles Use calibrated brightness-distance relationships to investigate expansion history.
Gravitational lensing Use the bending of light by gravity to map mass, including matter that is not directly visible.
Galaxy surveys and simulations Compare the observed cosmic web with predictions from models of structure formation.
Milestones in discovery

How our cosmic picture evolved

Space observatories and ground-based experiments have transformed cosmology from a largely theoretical subject into a precision science.

1920s

An expanding universe

Observations of distant galaxies helped establish that the universe is expanding, changing the picture of a static cosmos.

1965

The cosmic microwave background is discovered

The detection of pervasive microwave radiation provided powerful evidence for a hot, dense early universe.

1989–1993

COBE studies the early universe

NASA's Cosmic Background Explorer measured the microwave background spectrum and detected its tiny temperature variations.

1998

Accelerated expansion

Observations of distant Type Ia supernovae revealed that the expansion of the universe is accelerating.

2001–2010

WMAP maps the microwave background

NASA's Wilkinson Microwave Anisotropy Probe measured the background radiation with greater precision, helping constrain the universe's age and composition.

2009–2013 onward

Planck sharpens cosmological measurements

ESA's Planck mission produced detailed measurements of the cosmic microwave background, refining estimates of key cosmological parameters.

2015

Gravitational waves are directly detected

LIGO detected gravitational waves from a binary black-hole merger, opening a new way to observe the universe. LIGO is a ground-based observatory, not a space mission.

2021 onward

JWST explores distant galaxies

The James Webb Space Telescope observes early galaxies and distant cosmic environments, helping scientists investigate how structure developed over time.

2023 onward

Euclid maps the dark universe

ESA's Euclid mission surveys large portions of the sky to study the distribution of galaxies and the effects of dark matter and dark energy.

Evidence versus speculation

What do we actually know?

Cosmology includes well-tested observations, models that fit the evidence, and important ideas that still need stronger tests.

Strong evidence

The universe is expanding

Multiple observations show that, on very large scales, distances between unbound galaxies generally increase as the universe evolves.

Strong evidence

The early universe was hotter and denser

The cosmic microwave background and the measured abundance of light elements support a hot, dense early phase.

Strong evidence, unknown cause

Dark matter affects gravity

Several independent observations indicate additional gravitating matter, but its underlying particle or physical nature has not been identified.

Observed, unexplained

Expansion is accelerating

The acceleration is supported by astronomical observations. Dark energy is a common name for its cause, not a complete explanation of its nature.

Open question

What happened before the hot Big Bang phase?

Current evidence describes the early hot, dense universe very well, but a complete account of the earliest moments remains unresolved.

Speculative

Are there other universes?

Some theories allow a multiverse, but no confirmed observation currently establishes that other universes exist.
