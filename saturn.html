Inside the Planet

Understanding Saturn

Saturn is a gas giant with no solid surface like Earth. Its atmosphere gradually becomes denser and hotter deeper inside the planet.

A Giant Made Mostly of Gas

Saturn is composed primarily of hydrogen and helium. Its upper atmosphere contains broad bands of clouds and powerful winds, with storms that can grow to enormous sizes.

Scientists believe that Saturn has a dense interior surrounded by layers of hydrogen and helium. The exact structure of its deepest interior is still being studied.

Saturn is less dense than water on average, although this does not mean the planet could practically float in an ocean.

World of Wonders

What Makes Saturn Unique?

From its spectacular rings to its unusual weather, Saturn offers scientists a remarkable natural laboratory.

01 / RINGS

A Ring System Like No Other

Saturn's rings consist mostly of countless particles of water ice, mixed with rocky material and dust. The particles range from tiny grains to much larger objects.

02 / ATMOSPHERE

Extreme Winds

Powerful atmospheric currents race around Saturn. The planet also experiences large storms and changing cloud patterns.

03 / WEATHER

The Hexagon at the North Pole

A persistent six-sided jet-stream pattern surrounds Saturn's north polar region. Its unusual geometry has fascinated researchers for decades.

04 / INTERIOR

Deep Inside a Gas Giant

Saturn's internal pressure and temperature rise dramatically with depth. Studying its gravity helps scientists investigate its hidden interior.

05 / MAGNETISM

A Powerful Magnetic Environment

Saturn has a large magnetosphere that interacts with the solar wind, charged particles and material from its moons and rings.

06 / DISCOVERY

A System Full of Mysteries

Saturn's moons, ring structures and atmosphere continue to reveal clues about how planetary systems form and evolve.

Natural Satellites

Meet Saturn's Moons

Saturn's moons include worlds with underground oceans, methane lakes, ancient craters and active icy surfaces.

Titan

Saturn's largest moon has a dense nitrogen-rich atmosphere, rivers and lakes of liquid methane and ethane, and a complex organic environment.

Enceladus

This icy moon ejects water-rich plumes into space. Evidence suggests a global subsurface ocean beneath its frozen crust.

Rhea

Rhea is a heavily cratered icy moon with a bright surface and a history shaped by impacts across the Saturnian system.

Iapetus

Iapetus is known for its dramatic contrast between a dark hemisphere and a bright hemisphere, as well as its unusual equatorial ridge.

Exploration History

Humanity's Journey to Saturn

Spacecraft have transformed Saturn from a distant point of light into a complex planetary system we can study in detail.

Flagship Mission

Cassini–Huygens

NASA's Cassini spacecraft entered orbit around Saturn in 2004 and studied the planet, rings and moons for more than 13 years. The European Space Agency's Huygens probe landed on Titan in 2005, providing humanity's first close-up view from the surface of that distant world. Cassini's mission ended in 2017 with a controlled plunge into Saturn's atmosphere.

Voyager 1 and Voyager 2

The Voyager spacecraft flew past Saturn in 1980 and 1981. Their observations revealed new details about the planet's atmosphere, rings and moons.

What Comes Next?

Future exploration could investigate Titan's chemistry, Enceladus's ocean and the processes that shape Saturn's rings and satellites.
