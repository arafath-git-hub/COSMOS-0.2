Inside the Planet

Understanding Saturn

Saturn is a gas giant without a solid surface like Earth. Its atmosphere gradually becomes denser and hotter deeper inside.

A Giant Made Mostly of Gas

Saturn is composed primarily of hydrogen and helium. Its upper atmosphere contains broad cloud bands and powerful winds, along with storms that can grow to enormous sizes.

Scientists believe Saturn has a dense interior surrounded by layers of hydrogen and helium. The detailed structure of its deepest interior is still being studied.

Saturn is less dense than water on average, but this does not mean it could practically float in an ocean.

World of Wonders

What Makes Saturn Unique?

From spectacular rings to unusual weather, Saturn is a remarkable natural laboratory for planetary science.

01 / RINGS

A Ring System Like No Other

Saturn's rings consist mostly of countless particles of water ice, mixed with rocky material and dust. Their sizes range from tiny grains to much larger objects.

02 / ATMOSPHERE

Extreme Winds

Powerful atmospheric currents race around Saturn. The planet also experiences large storms and changing cloud patterns.

03 / WEATHER

The Polar Hexagon

A persistent six-sided jet-stream pattern surrounds Saturn's north polar region. Its unusual geometry continues to fascinate researchers.

04 / INTERIOR

Deep Inside a Gas Giant

Pressure and temperature rise dramatically with depth. Measurements of Saturn's gravity help scientists investigate its hidden interior.

05 / MAGNETISM

A Magnetic Environment

Saturn has a large magnetosphere that interacts with the solar wind, charged particles and material from its moons and rings.

06 / DISCOVERY

A System Full of Mysteries

Saturn's moons, rings and atmosphere continue to reveal clues about how planetary systems form and evolve.

Natural Satellites

Meet Saturn's Moons

Saturn's moons include worlds with underground oceans, methane lakes, ancient craters and active icy surfaces.

Titan

Saturn's largest moon has a dense nitrogen-rich atmosphere, lakes of liquid methane and ethane, and complex organic chemistry.

Enceladus

This icy moon ejects water-rich plumes into space. Evidence indicates a global subsurface ocean beneath its frozen crust.

Rhea

Rhea is a heavily cratered icy moon with a bright surface shaped by impacts throughout the Saturnian system.

Iapetus

Iapetus is known for its dramatic contrast between a dark hemisphere and a bright hemisphere, plus an unusual equatorial ridge.

Exploration History

Humanity's Journey to Saturn

Spacecraft have transformed Saturn from a distant point of light into a complex planetary system that scientists can study in detail.

Flagship Mission

Cassini–Huygens

NASA's Cassini spacecraft entered orbit around Saturn in 2004 and studied the planet, rings and moons for more than 13 years. The European Space Agency's Huygens probe landed on Titan in 2005, giving humanity its first close-up view from the surface of that distant world. Cassini's mission ended in 2017 with a controlled plunge into Saturn's atmosphere.

Voyager 1 and Voyager 2

The Voyager spacecraft flew past Saturn in 1980 and 1981. Their observations revealed new details about the planet's atmosphere, rings and moons.

Future Exploration

Future missions could investigate Titan's chemistry, Enceladus's ocean and the processes that shape Saturn's rings and satellites.
