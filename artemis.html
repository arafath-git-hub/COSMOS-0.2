Human Lunar Exploration

Artemis Program

NASA's Artemis Program is designed to return astronauts to the lunar surface, expand scientific exploration of the Moon, and develop capabilities for future human missions to Mars. It combines new spacecraft, launch systems, spacesuits, lunar technologies, and international partnerships.

Lead Agency NASA
Key Launch System Space Launch System
Crew Spacecraft Orion
Main Destination The Moon

What Is the Artemis Program?

Artemis is a series of missions intended to build a sustainable approach to human exploration of the Moon. The program focuses on scientific research, testing exploration technologies, and learning how people can live and work for longer periods away from Earth.

NASA is working with commercial partners and international space agencies on different elements of the program. The exact schedule and objectives of individual missions can evolve as hardware, testing, and operational plans develop.

Important Artemis Missions

  • Artemis I (2022): An uncrewed test flight of the Space Launch System rocket and Orion spacecraft around the Moon and back to Earth.
  • Artemis II: A planned crewed flight around the Moon to test Orion's systems with astronauts aboard.
  • Artemis III: Planned lunar surface exploration intended to advance human operations on the Moon. Its timing and architecture depend on program readiness.
  • Future Artemis missions: Intended to expand lunar science, test new technologies, and develop a longer-term human presence around and on the Moon.

Key Technologies

  • Space Launch System (SLS): A powerful rocket designed to launch Orion and its crew toward the Moon.
  • Orion spacecraft: Carries astronauts on deep-space journeys and supports their return to Earth.
  • Lunar landers: Commercially developed systems intended to transport astronauts between lunar orbit and the surface.
  • Spacesuits: Systems designed to support astronauts during lunar surface activities.
  • Lunar surface systems: Technologies intended to support mobility, communication, scientific work, and longer missions.

Why Return to the Moon?

The Moon preserves a record of Solar System history and provides a nearby environment for testing exploration systems. Studying lunar rocks, craters, and polar regions can improve our understanding of planetary formation and the history of Earth and the Moon.

Artemis also aims to develop experience in operating farther from Earth, where communication delays, radiation, limited supplies, and difficult surface conditions create additional challenges.

The Moon is both a scientific destination and a testing ground for technologies that may help enable future human exploration of Mars.

What Will Astronauts Study?

  • Lunar geology and the history of impacts across the Solar System.
  • Materials found in permanently shadowed polar regions, including possible water ice.
  • How lunar dust, radiation, and reduced gravity affect people and equipment.
  • Methods for conducting longer scientific expeditions on another world.

Mission Status and Schedule

Artemis is an ongoing exploration program. Mission schedules, crew assignments, landing plans, and technical designs may change as NASA and its partners complete testing and prepare flight systems. Consult NASA's official Artemis website for the latest confirmed schedule and mission information.
