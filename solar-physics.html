01 / Foundations

What is solar physics?

Solar physics investigates how the Sun works, how its activity changes, and how our star influences the wider heliosphere.

01 — INTERIOR

Energy from the core

In the Sun's core, nuclear fusion converts hydrogen into helium. Energy moves outward through the radiative and convective zones before escaping as sunlight and other radiation.

02 — MAGNETISM

A magnetic star

Electrically conducting plasma and the Sun's rotation help generate complex magnetic fields. Their changing structure powers many forms of solar activity.

03 — SPACE WEATHER

Beyond the Sun

Solar wind and eruptions travel through space. When conditions align, they can trigger auroras and disturb satellites, radio links and electrical infrastructure on Earth.

03 / Scientific Method

How do we study the Sun?

Solar scientists combine observations from Earth and space with physical models and direct measurements of particles and fields.

Observation and measurement

  • Visible-light imaging: tracks sunspots and surface structures.
  • Ultraviolet and X-ray imaging: reveals hot plasma and energetic events.
  • Spectroscopy: measures spectral lines to infer physical conditions.
  • Magnetograms: map magnetic-field information at the solar surface.
  • In-situ instruments: measure particles and magnetic fields in space.

Models and unanswered questions

  • Magnetohydrodynamics models plasma and magnetic-field interactions.
  • Computer simulations test possible mechanisms behind solar eruptions.
  • Helioseismology helps infer internal flows and structures.
  • Space-weather models estimate how solar disturbances may affect Earth.
  • Scientists compare model predictions with observations to refine explanations.
Scientific models are continually tested against measurements. A useful prediction is not the same as perfect certainty.

05 / Evidence Check

Facts, questions and misconceptions.

Solar physics distinguishes well-supported observations from unresolved problems and oversimplified claims.

The Sun is not a solid fireball.

It is primarily hot plasma, a state of matter containing free electrons and ions.

Sunspots are not holes.

They look dark because they are cooler than their surroundings, not because they are empty.

The corona is extremely hot.

Its temperature can reach millions of degrees. The exact mechanisms that heat it remain an active research area.

Solar storms do not all affect Earth equally.

Their effects depend on direction, speed, magnetic structure and Earth's response.

The solar cycle is not a precise clock.

Activity follows a roughly 11-year pattern, but the duration and strength of individual cycles vary.

Space weather is a real scientific field.

Monitoring solar activity helps assess risks to satellites, communications, navigation and power systems.
