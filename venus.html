02 / World profile

A planet of extremes

Venus is often called Earth's sister planet, but its atmosphere and climate create one of the most hostile environments in the Solar System.

PROFILE / A

A world hidden by clouds

Venus is wrapped in a dense atmosphere dominated by carbon dioxide, with clouds containing sulfuric acid droplets. These clouds reflect much of the sunlight that reaches the planet, making Venus exceptionally bright in Earth's sky.

PROFILE / B

A runaway greenhouse

The thick atmosphere traps heat through an intense greenhouse effect. This keeps the surface hot both day and night, despite Venus receiving less solar energy than Mercury.

03 / Atmospheric systems

What makes Venus unique?

SYSTEM 001

Crushing pressure

Surface atmospheric pressure is about 92 times Earth's sea-level pressure, similar to the pressure found roughly 900 metres underwater on Earth.

SYSTEM 002

Retrograde rotation

Venus rotates in the opposite direction to most planets. Its rotation is also extremely slow, making its solar day longer than its year.

SYSTEM 003

Super-rotating clouds

High-altitude winds circle Venus much faster than the planet itself rotates, carrying clouds around the world in only a few Earth days.

Atmosphere analysis

96.5%

Approximate carbon dioxide content of Venus's atmosphere

The atmosphere changes everything

Venus's atmosphere is much thicker than Earth's. Carbon dioxide traps outgoing heat, while clouds of sulfuric acid contribute to its reflective appearance. The result is a surface that remains extremely hot under nearly all conditions.

Understanding why Venus developed such a harsh climate can help scientists study planetary evolution and the long-term climate history of rocky worlds.

04 / Surface environment

A landscape shaped by volcanism

Thick clouds block ordinary visible-light views of the ground, so radar mapping has been essential for revealing the planet's surface.

GEOLOGY / 01

Vast volcanic plains

Much of Venus is covered by broad plains associated with volcanic activity. The planet also features mountains, impact craters, lava channels and unusual circular structures called coronae.

GEOLOGY / 02

A world still under investigation

Scientists continue to investigate how active Venus is today. Evidence of possible recent or ongoing volcanism has made the planet an important target for new spacecraft and future missions.

06 / Open questions

What scientists still want to know

Is Venus volcanically active today?

Fresh lava flows and changes in volcanic features could help researchers understand whether eruptions are happening now.

Did Venus once have liquid water?

Researchers are studying whether ancient Venus had conditions that could have supported stable bodies of liquid water.

How did its atmosphere evolve?

Atmospheric chemistry and the loss of water-related molecules may hold clues to Venus's dramatic climate history.

Could clouds reveal unexpected chemistry?

Venus's cloud layers remain an important place to investigate chemical processes and test hypotheses about its atmosphere.
