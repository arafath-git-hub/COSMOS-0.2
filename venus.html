VENUSThe Hidden World

Beneath a thick veil of golden clouds lies a world of crushing pressure, extreme heat and mysterious geology. Venus is similar in size to Earth, yet its environment is radically different.

A planet of extremes

Venus is the second planet from the Sun. Its dense carbon-dioxide atmosphere traps heat so efficiently that its surface is hotter than the surface of Mercury, even though Mercury is closer to the Sun.

02 / Planet profile

Inside the Venusian environment

Venus has a rocky interior, a thick atmosphere and a surface shaped by volcanic and tectonic processes. Many details of its interior remain uncertain because its surface is difficult to study directly.

Atmosphere

A crushing blanket of gas

Venus's atmosphere is composed mostly of carbon dioxide, with clouds containing sulfuric-acid droplets. Surface pressure is about 92 times Earth's sea-level pressure. The atmosphere drives a powerful greenhouse effect that maintains extraordinary heat.

  • Main gasCarbon dioxide (CO₂)
  • CloudsSulfuric-acid droplets
  • Surface pressureAbout 92 bar
  • Cloud-top appearanceBright and yellowish
Rotation & orbit

A strangely turning world

Venus rotates very slowly and in the opposite direction to most planets. This is called retrograde rotation. Its rotation period is longer than its orbital year, so a Venusian solar day and a Venusian sidereal day are not the same.

  • YearAbout 225 Earth days
  • Rotation directionRetrograde
  • Axial tiltAbout 177°
  • Natural moonsNone known
Surface & geology

A landscape hidden by clouds

Radar observations have revealed mountains, broad volcanic plains, impact craters and structures called coronae. Venus has many volcanic features, but scientists are still investigating how active its volcanism is today and how its surface has changed through time.

  • Surface typeRocky and volcanic
  • Major featuresPlains, mountains, coronae
  • Visible-light viewBlocked by thick clouds
  • Surface mappingRadar observations
Interior

A rocky planet like Earth?

Venus is expected to have a metallic core, a rocky mantle and a crust. Its exact internal structure is not yet well constrained. Unlike Earth, Venus does not have a confirmed global magnetic field generated by an active core dynamo.

  • Planet categoryTerrestrial
  • InteriorCore, mantle and crust
  • Global magnetic fieldNo confirmed intrinsic field
  • DensitySimilar to Earth's
Why is Venus so hot? Its atmosphere contains enormous amounts of carbon dioxide. This gas absorbs and re-emits infrared radiation, preventing much of the planet's heat from escaping into space. The result is an extreme greenhouse effect.
03 / Earth comparison

Earth's twin — but not its twin environment

Venus and Earth are similar in size and bulk composition, making their different climates one of planetary science's most important puzzles.

What they share

  • Planet typeRocky
  • Relative sizeVery similar
  • StructureRocky mantle and metallic core
  • Formation eraAbout 4.5 billion years ago

What makes them different

  • Surface climateExtreme heat on Venus
  • AtmosphereCO₂-dominated on Venus
  • Surface waterNo stable oceans known
  • Magnetic fieldEarth has a global field
04 / Space missions

How we explored Venus

Spacecraft have studied Venus using flybys, orbiters, radar mapping and short-lived landers. The hostile surface makes long-duration lander missions especially challenging.

1962

Mariner 2

NASA's spacecraft became the first successful mission to another planet, flying past Venus and measuring its environment.

First successful Venus flyby
1970

Venera 7

The Soviet spacecraft made the first successful transmission from the surface of another planet, providing direct measurements of Venus's harsh conditions.

First successful planetary landing data
1990–1994

Magellan

NASA's Magellan spacecraft used radar to map much of Venus's surface, revealing volcanic plains, mountains and other landforms beneath the clouds.

Radar surface mapping
2006–2014

Venus Express

ESA's orbiter studied Venus's atmosphere, clouds, temperature patterns and interaction with the solar wind.

Atmosphere and climate
2015–2025

Akatsuki

JAXA's Venus orbiter investigated atmospheric circulation, cloud patterns and weather. Its mission ended after contact with the spacecraft was lost in 2024; the mission was formally concluded in 2025.

Atmospheric dynamics
Upcoming missions

A new era of exploration

NASA's DAVINCI and VERITAS missions and ESA's EnVision are planned to investigate Venus's atmosphere, surface and geological history. Their schedules can change, so check official mission pages for the latest launch and status information.

05 / Open questions

What scientists still want to know

Venus holds clues about how rocky planets evolve and why worlds with similar beginnings can develop very different environments.

Was Venus ever habitable?

Scientists are investigating whether Venus may once have had liquid water and what conditions existed early in its history.

Are volcanoes active today?

Radar and atmospheric observations offer clues, but researchers need more evidence to understand the frequency and scale of modern volcanic activity.

Why did Venus evolve differently?

Comparing Venus and Earth may reveal how atmospheric chemistry, sunlight, water and geological processes shape planetary climates.
