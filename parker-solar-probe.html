Mission Overview

Why Study the Sun?

The Sun powers our solar system, but many processes in its outer atmosphere remain difficult to explain.

01 / OBJECTIVES

The Science Behind the Mission

Parker Solar Probe investigates how the solar corona becomes extremely hot and how the solar wind accelerates away from the Sun.

  • Study the structure and dynamics of the solar magnetic field.
  • Investigate how energy heats the corona.
  • Trace the origins and acceleration of the solar wind.
  • Study energetic particles released by solar activity.
02 / ENGINEERING

Built to Survive Extreme Heat

The spacecraft uses a specially designed thermal protection system to shield its instruments while it passes through the Sun's extraordinarily hot outer atmosphere.

  • A carbon-composite heat shield protects the spacecraft.
  • The shield's front surface can reach roughly 1,370°C.
  • Autonomous systems help maintain the correct orientation.
  • Solar panels provide electrical power during the mission.
Important distinction: The solar corona has a very high temperature, but it is extremely sparse. The spacecraft's heat shield protects it from the intense sunlight and thermal environment during close approaches.
Scientific Instruments

Inside the Mission

Four instrument suites measure particles, magnetic fields, electric fields and the Sun's surrounding environment.

01

FIELDS

Measures electric and magnetic fields, radio waves and plasma waves to investigate the electromagnetic environment near the Sun.

02

ISʘIS

Measures energetic electrons, protons and heavier ions associated with solar activity and energetic-particle events.

03

WISPR

The Wide-field Imager for Solar Probe captures images of the corona and inner heliosphere, revealing structures in the solar wind.

04

SWEAP

Measures properties of electrons, protons and helium ions in the solar wind, including their speeds, densities and temperatures.

Mission Timeline

A Journey Toward the Sun

Venus gravity assists gradually changed the spacecraft's orbit, allowing it to approach the Sun more closely.

August 2018

Launch

Parker Solar Probe launched aboard a Delta IV Heavy rocket, beginning its journey to study the Sun.

October 2018

First Venus Flyby

The spacecraft used Venus's gravity to adjust its orbit and begin moving closer to the Sun.

December 2021

First Corona Passage

NASA announced that Parker had flown through the Sun's corona, sampling the solar atmosphere directly.

December 2024

Record-Breaking Approach

On December 24, Parker reached approximately 6.1 million kilometres from the Sun's visible surface at a record-setting speed.

Scientific Discoveries

What Parker Is Teaching Us

Close-up measurements are revealing processes that cannot be understood fully from distant observations alone.

01

Direct Sampling of the Solar Corona

Parker became the first spacecraft to fly through the corona, providing direct measurements of plasma and magnetic fields in this extreme environment.

02

Magnetic Switchbacks

The spacecraft has studied sudden reversals in the direction of the solar magnetic field, known as switchbacks, which may help explain how energy moves through the solar wind.

03

Dust and the Inner Solar Environment

Measurements and images help scientists investigate dust, solar-wind structures and other material close to the Sun.

04

Understanding Space Weather

Studying how the solar wind and energetic particles originate can improve scientific understanding of solar storms that affect spacecraft, communications and power systems near Earth.

Mission Status

Continuing to Explore the Solar Environment

Parker Solar Probe was designed for repeated close encounters with the Sun. Its observations help researchers understand the corona, solar wind and energetic processes throughout the inner heliosphere. For the latest spacecraft status and mission updates, consult NASA's official mission website.
