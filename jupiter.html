Jupiter: The Giant.

A world of colossal storms, striped clouds and a powerful magnetic field. Jupiter is the largest planet in our Solar System—and its icy moons may hold clues to whether life-friendly environments exist beyond Earth.

01 / At a Glance

Jupiter by the Numbers

Jupiter is so massive that it contains more than twice the mass of all the other Solar System planets combined.

A Planet of Extraordinary Scale

Jupiter is the fifth planet from the Sun and the largest planet in the Solar System. Its enormous atmosphere, rapid rotation and strong gravity make it one of the most complex worlds for planetary scientists to investigate.

Unlike Earth, Jupiter has no known solid surface on which a spacecraft could land. Its visible cloud tops are the upper layers of a deep atmosphere dominated by hydrogen and helium.

02 / Inside the Giant

A Planet Without a Solid Surface

Jupiter's visible clouds are only the upper layers of a vast, deep atmosphere.

01 / ATMOSPHERE

Clouds of Hydrogen and Helium

Jupiter's atmosphere consists mostly of hydrogen and helium, with smaller amounts of methane, ammonia, water vapour and other compounds. Its pale zones and darker belts are shaped by powerful winds, chemistry and differences in cloud altitude.

02 / GREAT RED SPOT

A Storm Larger Than Earth

The Great Red Spot is a giant, long-lived anticyclonic storm in Jupiter's southern hemisphere. It has been observed for centuries, although its size and appearance have changed over time.

03 / INTERIOR

Pressure Beyond Imagination

Deeper inside Jupiter, pressure rises until hydrogen behaves like an electrically conducting metallic fluid. Scientists are still investigating the planet's deep interior and central region.

04 / MAGNETOSPHERE

A Powerful Magnetic Environment

Jupiter has the strongest planetary magnetic field in the Solar System. It traps charged particles and creates intense radiation belts that make spacecraft operations a major engineering challenge.

05 / RINGS

Faint Rings in the Dark

Jupiter has a faint ring system made primarily of dust. The rings are much less conspicuous than Saturn's and are replenished by material knocked off small inner moons by impacts.

06 / WEATHER

Winds, Waves and Giant Vortices

Jupiter's atmosphere contains jet streams, turbulent cloud systems and storms of many sizes. Heat rising from its interior also helps drive its atmospheric activity.

Did You Know?

Jupiter rotates so quickly that its shape is slightly flattened at the poles and bulges around the equator.

03 / Worlds of Their Own

Meet the Four Galilean Moons

Galileo Galilei observed these four large moons in 1610. Each is a different kind of world.

Io

A volcanically active world, powered by tidal heating as Jupiter and neighbouring moons tug on it.

Europa

An icy moon with strong evidence for a salty ocean beneath its frozen surface—an important target in the search for habitable environments.

Ganymede

The largest moon in the Solar System. It has its own intrinsic magnetic field and evidence of a subsurface ocean.

Callisto

A heavily cratered ancient world that may also contain a hidden ocean beneath its icy exterior.

Could an Ocean Moon Support Life?

Europa, Ganymede and Callisto are important targets for studying hidden oceans. Scientists have not discovered life there; they are investigating whether conditions suitable for life might exist.

04 / Robotic Explorers

How We Explore Jupiter

Every spacecraft adds a new piece to our understanding of the giant planet and its moons.

1973–75

Pioneer 10 and Pioneer 11

Pioneer 10 made the first close spacecraft encounter with Jupiter in 1973. Pioneer 11 followed in 1974, returning observations that helped prepare the way for later missions.

1979

Voyager 1 and Voyager 2

The Voyager flybys revealed remarkable details of Jupiter's clouds, rings and moons, including Io's active volcanoes.

1995–2003

Galileo

NASA's Galileo spacecraft orbited Jupiter, studied its large moons and delivered an atmospheric probe into the planet's clouds in 1995. Its mission ended in 2003 with a controlled plunge into Jupiter.

2016–present*

Juno

NASA's Juno spacecraft entered Jupiter orbit in 2016. It investigates the planet's gravity, magnetic field, deep atmosphere, auroras and interior structure, while also observing its moons.

2030 planned

Europa Clipper — NASA

Launched in October 2024, Europa Clipper is travelling toward Jupiter. It is planned to arrive in 2030 and make repeated close flybys of Europa to investigate its ice shell, ocean and potential habitability.

2031 planned

JUICE — European Space Agency

ESA's Jupiter Icy Moons Explorer launched in 2023. Its planned arrival in 2031 will begin a detailed study of Jupiter and its icy moons, with a major focus on Ganymede.

*Mission status and future arrival dates can change. Future dates are mission plans, not completed events.
