Hubble Space Telescope

A Window Into The Universe

Operating above Earth's atmosphere, the Hubble Space Telescope captures extraordinary views of galaxies, nebulae, stars, and planets. For decades, its observations have helped astronomers investigate the universe's history, structure, and evolution.

01 / Introduction

What Is Hubble?

The Hubble Space Telescope is a space-based astronomical observatory developed through a collaboration between NASA and the European Space Agency. It was launched aboard the Space Shuttle Discovery on April 24, 1990, and deployed into orbit the following day.

Hubble observes the universe primarily in ultraviolet, visible, and near-infrared light. Above most of Earth's atmosphere, it can obtain sharp observations without much of the blurring and absorption that affect ground-based telescopes.

Its 2.4-metre primary mirror gathers light from distant cosmic objects. Sensitive scientific instruments then record that light to produce images and measurements used in astronomical research.

Hubble is not simply a camera in space. It is a scientific observatory that turns light from the universe into evidence about how cosmic objects form, change, and evolve.
02 / Technology

How Does Hubble Work?

Hubble points toward astronomical targets selected by scientists. Its mirror collects incoming light and directs it toward scientific instruments designed for different kinds of observations.

Light Collection

The primary mirror gathers light from stars, galaxies, nebulae, and other objects, allowing the telescope to study faint sources far beyond our Solar System.

Scientific Instruments

Cameras and spectrographs measure light, revealing information about an object's brightness, structure, composition, and physical conditions.

Orbital Observations

Hubble circles Earth in low Earth orbit, allowing it to observe astronomical targets from above most atmospheric interference.

Data on Earth

Observations are transmitted to the ground, where researchers process the data, analyze the results, and share scientific findings with the world.

03 / Scientific Impact

Major Discoveries and Contributions

Hubble has contributed to thousands of scientific investigations. Its long operating history has made it especially valuable for studying objects across different distances and stages of cosmic evolution.

  • Expansion of the universe: Helped refine measurements of the universe's expansion rate, contributing to research into its history and evolution.
  • Distant galaxies: Observed galaxies across enormous distances, helping scientists investigate how galaxies looked in the early universe.
  • Star formation: Captured detailed views of nebulae and regions where stars form, evolve, and influence their surroundings.
  • Black holes and galaxies: Provided important observations of galactic centres and the relationships between galaxies and their central black holes.
  • Exoplanets and planetary science: Contributed observations of planets beyond our Solar System and studied atmospheric and weather phenomena on Solar System planets.
  • Deep-field observations: Revealed thousands of galaxies in tiny regions of the sky, showing how much the universe contains beyond what is visible to the unaided eye.
Hubble's deep-field images demonstrate that a seemingly empty patch of dark sky can contain thousands of galaxies, each with its own stars and cosmic history.
04 / Cosmic Exploration

Why Is Hubble Important?

Hubble transformed astronomy by delivering detailed observations over several decades. Because researchers can revisit objects and compare observations taken years apart, they can investigate changes in stars, planetary atmospheres, nebulae, and other astronomical systems.

Its discoveries have influenced research across cosmology, stellar evolution, galaxy formation, and planetary science. Hubble has also helped make astronomy more accessible through its widely shared images of the universe.

Its observations complement those of other observatories, including the James Webb Space Telescope. Hubble's ultraviolet and visible-light capabilities provide a different perspective from Webb's powerful infrared observations.

05 / Mission Status

Is Hubble Still Active?

Hubble remains an operating scientific observatory. However, the telescope and its supporting systems have aged, so engineers carefully manage its equipment and operating conditions.

Its observing schedule and technical status can change. For the latest operational updates, new discoveries, and mission announcements, consult NASA's official Hubble mission website.

Mission status is time-sensitive. Always check official mission updates for the latest information about Hubble's operations.
