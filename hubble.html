Space Astronomy Observatory

Hubble Space Telescope

The Hubble Space Telescope is one of the most influential astronomical observatories ever built. Operating above Earth's atmosphere, Hubble captures sharp images and measurements of galaxies, nebulae, stars, planets, and other cosmic objects.

Space Agencies NASA and ESA
Launch Date April 24, 1990
Orbit Low Earth orbit
Primary Role Observing the universe

What Is Hubble?

Hubble is a space telescope that observes the universe primarily in ultraviolet, visible, and near-infrared light. Because it operates above Earth's atmosphere, it avoids much of the atmospheric blurring and absorption that affect ground-based observations.

The observatory has a primary mirror about 2.4 metres across. Its scientific instruments collect light and turn it into images and measurements that astronomers use to investigate cosmic objects.

How Does Hubble Work?

Hubble points toward selected astronomical targets and collects light with its mirror. Its instruments record this light at different wavelengths, allowing scientists to study an object's brightness, shape, temperature, motion, and chemical properties.

Hubble travels around Earth in low Earth orbit. Astronomers and engineers plan observations, monitor the observatory, and analyze the data returned to the ground.

Major Discoveries and Contributions

  • Helped improve measurements of the universe's expansion rate.
  • Observed galaxies at great distances, allowing scientists to study earlier stages of cosmic history.
  • Captured detailed views of star-forming nebulae and the evolution of stars.
  • Contributed to research on black holes and the structure of galaxies.
  • Observed planets and other objects within our Solar System.
  • Produced deep-field images revealing thousands of galaxies in small regions of the sky.
Hubble's deep-field observations show that even a tiny patch of dark sky can contain thousands of distant galaxies.

Why Is Hubble Important?

Hubble has helped transform astronomy by providing detailed observations over decades. Its long operating lifetime allows researchers to compare observations across time and investigate how stars, galaxies, and other objects change.

Hubble also works alongside other observatories, including the James Webb Space Telescope. Their different instruments and wavelength ranges provide complementary views of the universe.

Is Hubble Still Active?

Hubble remains a scientific space observatory, although its aging systems and instruments require careful management. Its current operating condition and observing plans may change, so consult NASA's official mission page for the latest updates.
