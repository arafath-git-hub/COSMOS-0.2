Outer Planet Exploration

Juno Mission

NASA's Juno spacecraft was designed to investigate Jupiter, the largest planet in our Solar System. By studying its atmosphere, gravity, magnetic field, and interior, Juno helps scientists understand how Jupiter formed and how the Solar System evolved.

Space Agency NASA
Launch Date August 5, 2011
Arrival at Jupiter July 4, 2016
Destination Jupiter and its moons

What Is the Juno Spacecraft?

Juno is a robotic spacecraft powered by large solar arrays. It follows a highly elliptical orbit around Jupiter, allowing it to make close scientific passes while reducing the time spent in some of the planet's most intense radiation regions.

The spacecraft carries instruments that measure Jupiter's atmosphere, magnetic field, gravity, energetic particles, and auroras. Its camera, JunoCam, also provides detailed images for scientific study and public engagement.

Mission Objectives

  • Determine how much water and other heavy elements are present in Jupiter's atmosphere.
  • Investigate the planet's deep structure and possible core.
  • Map Jupiter's powerful magnetic field and investigate its auroras.
  • Study atmospheric circulation, storms, clouds, and the famous Great Red Spot.
  • Improve understanding of how giant planets form and evolve.

Important Discoveries

  • Revealed that Jupiter's atmosphere has complex structures extending deep below its visible clouds.
  • Provided new measurements of Jupiter's gravity and magnetic field.
  • Improved scientists' understanding of the Great Red Spot and powerful polar cyclones.
  • Collected close-up observations of Jupiter's large moons, including Ganymede, Europa, and Io.
  • Returned detailed images that show Jupiter's atmosphere in remarkable complexity.
Jupiter's enormous size and powerful gravity make it an important natural laboratory for studying how giant planets develop.

Why Study Jupiter?

Jupiter preserves clues about the early Solar System. Understanding its composition and internal structure helps researchers test theories about how planets formed from the disk of gas and dust around the young Sun.

Studying Jupiter's magnetic environment and its moons also helps scientists understand a wide range of planetary processes.

Mission Status

Juno's mission has been extended beyond its original prime mission. Its operational status and planned activities can change, so check NASA's official mission page for the latest confirmed information.
