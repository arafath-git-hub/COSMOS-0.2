Journey to the largest planet in our Solar System. NASA's Juno spacecraft studies Jupiter's atmosphere, magnetic field, gravity, interior structure and powerful auroras to uncover how this giant world formed and evolved.

The Story

Why We Sent Juno

Jupiter preserves important clues about the early history of our planetary system.

Understanding Jupiter's Origins

Jupiter formed from material left over during the birth of the Sun and planets. Its composition and interior structure can help scientists understand the conditions that existed in the early Solar System.

Juno measures Jupiter's gravity and magnetic field to investigate what lies beneath its thick cloud layers.

A Journey Through Extreme Conditions

Jupiter has an enormous atmosphere, powerful magnetic fields, intense radiation and spectacular auroras. Studying these phenomena helps researchers understand how giant planets work.

Juno follows a highly elliptical orbit designed to gather science data while limiting its exposure to Jupiter's intense radiation environment.

Science Objectives

What Juno Investigates

Multiple instruments work together to reveal the hidden processes shaping the giant planet.

01 / ATMOSPHERE

Clouds and Storms

Juno investigates atmospheric composition, temperature, cloud structure and deep weather systems, including the famous Great Red Spot.

02 / MAGNETISM

Magnetic Field and Auroras

Measurements reveal the structure of Jupiter's powerful magnetic field and help scientists investigate the energetic particles responsible for its brilliant polar auroras.

03 / INTERIOR

Gravity and Internal Structure

Tiny changes in the spacecraft's motion help scientists map Jupiter's gravity field and investigate how its mass is distributed deep inside the planet.

Beyond the Clouds

Every Orbit Reveals Another Piece of Jupiter's Story.

Juno's observations help scientists build a more complete picture of giant planets and improve our understanding of planetary systems beyond our own.
