Fields of discovery

How scientists investigate space

Researchers combine observations, experiments, mathematical models, and spacecraft data to test ideas about how the cosmos works.

FIELD 01

Planetary Science

Worlds & geology

Studies the formation, composition, surfaces, atmospheres, interiors, and evolution of planets, moons, asteroids, and comets.

FIELD 02

Astrobiology

Life beyond Earth

Investigates life's origins, the limits of life on Earth, and whether other worlds could have environments suitable for life.

FIELD 03

Exoplanets

Other star systems

Studies planets beyond our Solar System, including their sizes, orbits, atmospheres, and potential habitability.

FIELD 04

Cosmology

The universe

Investigates the universe's origin, expansion, large-scale structure, history, and possible future using observations and physical models.

FIELD 05

Black Holes

Extreme gravity

Explores regions where gravity is so strong that beyond the event horizon, light cannot escape, and tests physics under extreme conditions.

FIELD 06

Solar Physics

Our star

Studies the Sun's magnetic field, flares, solar wind, and space weather that can affect satellites, astronauts, and technology on Earth.

FIELD 07

Galaxies & Stars

Cosmic evolution

Examines how stars form and evolve, how galaxies grow, and how stellar processes create and distribute many chemical elements.

FIELD 08

Asteroids & Comets

Ancient material

Studies primitive bodies that preserve clues about the early Solar System and the ingredients from which planets formed.

FIELD 09

Space Technology

Tools for discovery

Develops instruments, sensors, spacecraft, and data systems that allow scientists to investigate places too distant or dangerous for people.

From evidence to discovery

How does space research work?

Science is a process, not just a collection of facts. Researchers ask a clear question, gather observations, develop a testable explanation, compare predictions with evidence, and revise their explanation when new results demand it.

Space scientists use telescopes, spacecraft, laboratory experiments, computer simulations, and measurements from Earth. Every method has limits, so independent evidence and careful uncertainty estimates matter.

Open questions

What do we still not know?

These are active scientific questions. Some have promising hypotheses, but none should be treated as settled without strong evidence.

Is there life beyond Earth?

No confirmed evidence of extraterrestrial life has been found. Mars, icy moons, and exoplanets are among the places researchers investigate.

What are dark matter and dark energy?

Observations indicate that ordinary matter alone cannot explain the universe's structure and expansion. The underlying nature of dark matter and dark energy remains unknown.

How did the universe begin?

The Big Bang model describes the universe's evolution from an early hot, dense state. What, if anything, preceded that state is still an open question.

Are we alone in the universe?

Scientists search for possible biosignatures and investigate how common potentially habitable planets may be. Habitability does not itself prove that life exists.

How do planets become habitable?

Atmospheres, chemistry, energy sources, geology, and a star's behavior can all influence whether a world might support life.

What happens inside black holes?

Observations test our theories of gravity, but the deepest interior physics of black holes remains beyond direct experimental access.
