01 / Program Overview

The Mission That Took Humanity to the Moon

Announced in 1961, Apollo developed the spacecraft, launch systems and operational skills needed to send astronauts to the lunar surface and return them safely to Earth.

What Was the Apollo Program?

Apollo was NASA's historic effort to achieve crewed lunar exploration. It included uncrewed tests, crewed test flights, astronaut training, spacecraft development and scientific investigations on the Moon.

The Saturn V rocket launched Apollo spacecraft toward the Moon. The Command Module carried the crew during the journey home, while the Lunar Module was designed to land on the Moon and return astronauts to lunar orbit.

The program combined engineering, navigation, computing, communications and human teamwork on a remarkable scale.

Core Program Objectives

01

Reach the Moon

Develop reliable systems for crewed journeys beyond Earth orbit.

02

Land Astronauts

Enable astronauts to descend to and work on the lunar surface.

03

Return Safely

Bring the crew back to Earth after their lunar expeditions.

04

Advance Science

Collect lunar samples and conduct experiments beyond Earth.

02 / Mission Timeline

Milestones That Changed History

From early crewed lunar exploration to the final Apollo Moon landing, each mission contributed to a new understanding of the Moon.

1961

The Lunar Goal Is Announced

The United States established the goal of landing astronauts on the Moon and returning them safely to Earth before the end of the decade.

1968

Apollo 8 Orbits the Moon

Apollo 8 became the first crewed mission to travel to and orbit the Moon, demonstrating critical capabilities for later landings.

1969

Apollo 11 Lands on the Moon

On 20 July 1969, the Apollo 11 Lunar Module landed on the Moon. Neil Armstrong and Buzz Aldrin walked on its surface while Michael Collins remained in lunar orbit.

1970

Apollo 13 Faces an Emergency

An oxygen tank failure forced the crew to abandon the planned lunar landing. The astronauts returned safely to Earth after a complex recovery effort.

1971–1972

Extended Lunar Science

Apollo 15, 16 and 17 expanded surface exploration. Astronauts used the Lunar Roving Vehicle on these later missions to investigate a wider range of lunar terrain.

1972

Apollo 17: The Final Lunar Landing

Apollo 17 completed the final crewed Moon landing of the Apollo era in December 1972, extending the program's scientific legacy.

03 / Scientific Discoveries

What Apollo Taught Us About the Moon

The missions returned lunar samples, recorded observations and deployed experiments that helped researchers investigate the Moon's origin, geology and environment.

DISCOVERY 01

Lunar Rock and Soil

Astronauts collected samples from different lunar locations. Laboratory analysis revealed important details about the Moon's composition and geological history.

DISCOVERY 02

Ancient Lunar History

Lunar samples helped scientists reconstruct major events in the Moon's history and understand its relationship with Earth.

DISCOVERY 03

Surface Geology

Photographs, field observations and sample collection improved scientific understanding of craters, mountains and lunar plains.

DISCOVERY 04

Experiments on the Moon

Apollo crews deployed scientific equipment to investigate aspects of the lunar environment and interior.

DISCOVERY 05

Human Exploration

The missions demonstrated how astronauts could navigate, work, collect samples and conduct scientific tasks in a challenging environment beyond Earth.

DISCOVERY 06

Technology and Engineering

Apollo advanced guidance, computing, communications, life-support systems and the engineering of complex crewed space missions.

Why Apollo Still Matters

Apollo established a foundation of lunar samples, scientific data, engineering experience and operational knowledge. Researchers continue to study its findings, while modern lunar exploration builds on lessons learned during the program.

04 / Spacecraft Technology

Engineering a Journey to Another World

Apollo depended on multiple spacecraft and a powerful launch vehicle working together throughout the mission.

The Saturn V Rocket

Saturn V was the heavy-lift launch vehicle used for Apollo lunar missions. Its stages provided the thrust needed to lift the spacecraft from Earth and send it on a trajectory toward the Moon.

Its design represented a major achievement in propulsion, structural engineering, flight control and launch operations.

Command and Lunar Modules

The Command and Service Modules supported the crew's journey, while the Lunar Module carried astronauts between lunar orbit and the surface.

After lunar operations, the astronauts returned to the Command Module for the journey back to Earth.

05 / Mission Status

A Completed Program, An Ongoing Legacy

The Apollo lunar missions are complete. The program's spacecraft are no longer conducting crewed lunar flights, and the equipment left on the Moon is part of the historical record of exploration.

Scientists continue to analyze Apollo samples and data. The knowledge gained through the program remains important to lunar science, spacecraft engineering and the planning of future human exploration missions.
