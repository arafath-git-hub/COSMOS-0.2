Planetary Science

Discover how planets form, what their surfaces and atmospheres reveal, and how scientists explore worlds across our Solar System.

What is Planetary Science?

Planetary science is the study of planets, moons, dwarf planets, asteroids, comets, and other planetary bodies. It combines astronomy, geology, chemistry, physics, and atmospheric science to understand how these worlds formed and how they have changed over time.

Scientists study Earth alongside other worlds to understand what makes planets similar or different and how planetary environments evolve.

Major Research Areas

Planetary Surfaces

Investigate craters, mountains, volcanoes, dunes, canyons, and icy landscapes to learn about a world's geological history.

Atmospheres & Climate

Compare atmospheric gases, clouds, storms, winds, and temperatures across planets and their moons.

Planet Formation

Study how dust and gas around young stars gradually form planets and smaller objects.

Planetary Interiors

Use gravity, magnetic fields, and seismic measurements to infer the internal structures of planetary bodies.

Moons & Icy Worlds

Explore natural satellites, including worlds that may contain oceans beneath their icy surfaces.

Small Solar System Bodies

Examine asteroids and comets to understand the materials left over from the Solar System's formation.

How Do Scientists Study Planets?

  • Telescopes: Observe planets from Earth and space using different wavelengths of light.
  • Orbiters: Map surfaces and measure atmospheres, gravity, magnetic fields, and other properties.
  • Landers and rovers: Investigate planetary surfaces directly using cameras and scientific instruments.
  • Laboratory analysis: Examine meteorites and returned samples to identify minerals and chemical compounds.
  • Computer models: Test explanations of planetary formation, climate, and geological processes.

Important Scientific Questions

How did planets form?

Researchers investigate how material in young planetary systems gathered into planets with different sizes and compositions.

Why are worlds different?

Planetary mass, distance from the Sun, chemistry, and geological history all influence how a world develops.

Could other worlds support life?

Scientists examine water, energy sources, and environmental conditions to assess whether a world may be habitable.
