Know your universe

Inside the Black Hole Family

Black holes are classified in part by mass. Their origins and growth histories are active areas of astronomical research.

Category 01

Stellar-mass Black Holes

These can form when a sufficiently massive star exhausts its nuclear fuel and its core collapses. Astronomers detect them through their effects on nearby matter, stars and spacetime.

Category 02

Intermediate-mass Black Holes

These occupy a mass range between typical stellar-mass and supermassive black holes. Their population, formation pathways and evolutionary role remain important research questions.

Category 03

Supermassive Black Holes

These contain millions to billions of solar masses and are found in the centers of many galaxies. Their growth is linked to the evolution of galaxies and energetic active galactic nuclei.

Science highlights

What Makes Black Holes Extraordinary?

Explore some key ideas that help scientists investigate these extreme objects.

01

The Event Horizon

The event horizon is a boundary beyond which signals cannot escape to distant observers. It is not a solid surface.

02

Gravitational Lensing

Gravity curves the path of light. A black hole can distort images of background objects and produce ring-like features.

03

Accretion Disks

Gas and dust can heat up as they orbit and spiral toward a black hole, releasing radiation that telescopes can detect.
