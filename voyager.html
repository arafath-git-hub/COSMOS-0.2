Voyager Missions

Voyager 1 and Voyager 2 are NASA spacecraft launched in 1977 to explore the outer Solar System. Their remarkable journeys transformed our understanding of the giant planets and eventually carried both spacecraft into interstellar space.

Organization NASA
Launch Year 1977
Spacecraft Voyager 1 and 2
Mission Region Outer Solar System and beyond

What Are the Voyager Spacecraft?

Voyager 1 launched on September 5, 1977, and Voyager 2 launched on August 20, 1977. Each spacecraft carries scientific instruments to measure magnetic fields, charged particles, plasma waves, and other features of space.

The spacecraft also carry a Golden Record: a message from Earth containing sounds, music, greetings, and images intended to represent life and culture on our planet.

Voyager 1

Voyager 1 flew past Jupiter in 1979 and Saturn in 1980. Its observations revealed details about the planets, their moons, rings, and magnetic environments. It later traveled outward and entered interstellar space in 2012, becoming the first spacecraft to do so.

Voyager 1 studies the environment beyond the heliosphere, the vast region influenced by the Sun's solar wind.

Voyager 2

Voyager 2 visited Jupiter and Saturn before continuing to Uranus in 1986 and Neptune in 1989. It remains the only spacecraft to have visited both of these ice giant planets up close. Voyager 2 entered interstellar space in 2018.

Major Discoveries

  • Observed active volcanoes on Jupiter's moon Io.
  • Investigated the complex rings and moons of Saturn.
  • Revealed unexpected atmospheric and magnetic features at Uranus.
  • Studied Neptune's powerful winds and its moon Triton.
  • Provided the first direct measurements of the interstellar environment beyond the heliosphere.

Are the Voyagers Still Working?

Both spacecraft have operated far longer than their original planetary exploration missions. Their electrical power decreases over time, so NASA has had to turn off some instruments and systems to conserve energy. Their operational status can change as engineers manage the aging spacecraft.

They communicate with Earth across enormous distances, where radio signals take many hours to travel one way.

Why Do These Missions Matter?

The Voyager missions expanded humanity's view of the outer planets and opened a new chapter in the exploration of interstellar space. They continue to help scientists understand how the Sun interacts with the space between stars.
