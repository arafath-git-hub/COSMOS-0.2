Voyager Missions

Launched in 1977, Voyager 1 and Voyager 2 transformed our understanding of the outer Solar System. After exploring the giant planets, both spacecraft journeyed beyond the heliosphere into interstellar space.

Humanity's farthest-reaching robotic explorers
Organization NASA
Launch Year 1977
Spacecraft Voyager 1 and 2
Mission Region Interstellar Space

What Are the Voyager Spacecraft?

Voyager 1 launched on September 5, 1977, and Voyager 2 launched on August 20, 1977. Both spacecraft carry scientific instruments designed to measure magnetic fields, charged particles, plasma waves, and other properties of their space environment.

Each spacecraft also carries a Golden Record containing selected sounds, music, greetings, and images from Earth. It was designed as a symbolic message representing life and culture on our planet.

The Voyager missions were originally designed to investigate the outer planets. Their extraordinary longevity has extended that exploration into the space between stars.

Voyager 1

Voyager 1 flew past Jupiter in 1979 and Saturn in 1980. Its observations revealed new details about the planets, their moons, rings, and magnetic environments. It later continued outward and crossed the heliopause in August 2012, becoming the first spacecraft to enter interstellar space.

Mission milestone: Voyager 1 became the first human-made spacecraft confirmed to enter interstellar space, beyond the region dominated by the Sun's solar wind.

Voyager 2

Voyager 2 visited Jupiter and Saturn before continuing to Uranus in 1986 and Neptune in 1989. It remains the only spacecraft to have visited both ice giant planets up close. Voyager 2 crossed the heliopause in November 2018 and entered interstellar space.

Its unusual planetary route provided scientists with close-up views of worlds that have not been revisited by another spacecraft on the same scale.

Major Discoveries

  • Observed active volcanoes on Jupiter's moon Io.
  • Investigated the complex rings and moons of Saturn.
  • Revealed unexpected atmospheric and magnetic features at Uranus.
  • Studied Neptune's powerful winds and its unusual moon Triton.
  • Measured the interstellar environment beyond the heliosphere.
  • Expanded our understanding of how the Sun interacts with the space between stars.

Are the Voyagers Still Working?

Both spacecraft have operated far beyond their original planetary exploration missions. Their radioisotope power sources gradually produce less electricity, so engineers have switched off selected instruments and systems to conserve the remaining power.

Their operational status and available instruments can change as NASA engineers manage the aging spacecraft. Because they are now extremely far from Earth, radio signals take many hours to travel one way.

The spacecraft are not receiving regular physical maintenance. Engineers must operate them remotely, using limited power and commands sent across immense distances.

Why Do These Missions Matter?

The Voyager missions reshaped humanity's view of the outer planets and opened a new chapter in the exploration of interstellar space. Their observations continue to help scientists investigate the boundary between the Sun's influence and the broader environment of our galaxy.

Voyager also demonstrates how carefully designed spacecraft can keep delivering scientific discoveries decades after launch.
